What the Discriminator Restores

Every finetune we ran without an adversarial term lost roughly 40% of the image's high-frequency energy, and no amount of extra training brought it back — 21 epochs moved it from 59% to 61%. One epoch with a discriminator restored it to 104%, matching Stability's released model. The two middle columns below are the same run, one epoch apart, differing only in whether disc_start had been passed.

PSNR moves the wrong way, which is why this was easy to miss. The GAN arm is a full dB worse on PSNR than the no-GAN arm it beats on every other measure — and Stability's own ft-EMA scores below both. Optimising reconstruction accuracy on 20k faces reliably destroyed texture.

The discriminator is pretrained, not random. CompVis' released kl-f8 is a full Lightning training checkpoint, so it still carries its loss.discriminator.* weights from step 246,803. That is what makes disc_start: 200 safe; upstream's default of 50001 exists because a randomly-initialised discriminator emits noise for thousands of steps.

Caveat: this is two epochs. Whether it holds or overshoots toward over-sharpening as training continues is not yet measured. Note sd-vae-ft-mse reads 132% on the gown — adversarially-trained decoders can over-produce texture.
